Example 1 — Product Mix LP (Problem Statement)

A manufacturing company is engaged in producing three types of products: A, B and C. The production department produces, each day, components sufficient to make 50 units of A, 25 units of B and 30 units of C. The management is confronted with the problem of optimizing the daily production of products in the assembly department where only 100 man‑hours are available daily to assemble the products. The following additional information is available.

The company has a daily order commitment for 20 units of product A and a total of 15 units of products B and C combined. Formulate this problem as an LP model so as to maximize the total profit.

Example 2 — Bottling Plant LP (Problem Statement)

A production planner in a soft drink plant has two bottling machines, A and B. Machine A is designed for 8‑ounce bottles and B for 16‑ounce bottles, but each can also be used for the other bottle size with some loss of efficiency. The manufacturing data is as follows:

Machine8‑ounce Bottles16‑ounce Bottles
A100/minute40/minute

The machines can be run 8 hours per day, 5 days per week (2,400 minutes/week each). Profit is 15 paise (Rs 0.15) on an 8‑ounce bottle and 25 paise (Rs 0.25) on a 16‑ounce bottle. Weekly production cannot exceed 300,000 bottles, and the market can absorb at most 25,000 8‑ounce bottles and 7,000 16‑ounce bottles per week. Formulate this problem as an LP model to maximize total profit.

Example 3 — Two-Plant Production LP (Problem Statement)

A company has two plants, each of which produces and supplies two products: A and B. The plants can each work up to 16 hours a day. In plant 1, it takes three hours to prepare and pack 1,000 gallons of A and one hour to prepare and pack one quintal of B. In plant 2, it takes two hours to prepare and pack 1,000 gallons of A and 1.5 hours to prepare and pack a quintal of B. In plant 1, it costs Rs 15,000 to prepare and pack 1,000 gallons of A and Rs 28,000 to prepare and pack a quintal of B, whereas these costs are Rs 18,000 and Rs 26,000, respectively, in plant 2. The company is obliged to produce daily at least 10 thousand gallons of A and 8 quintals of B.

Formulate this problem as an LP model to find out how the company should organize its production so that the required amounts of the two products are obtained at minimum cost.

Decision variables: x₁, x₂ = '000 gallons of A produced in plants 1 and 2; x₃, x₄ = quintals of B produced in plants 1 and 2.
Minimize Z = 15000x₁ + 18000x₂ + 28000x₃ + 26000x₄
s.t. 3x₁+x₂ ≤ 16; 2x₃+1.5x₄ ≤ 16; x₁+x₂ ≥ 10; x₃+x₄ ≥ 8; x₁,x₂,x₃,x₄ ≥ 0.
Note: the two product lines share no constraint and don't interact in the objective, so this 4‑variable problem is really two independent 2‑variable LPs. Plotted below is the Product A subproblem (x₁→x, x₂→y) — minimum at (3, 7), Z = 171,000. The Product B subproblem (minimize 28000x₃+26000x₄ s.t. 2x₃+1.5x₄≤16, x₃+x₄≥8) solves the same way, with its own minimum at (x₃,x₄) = (0, 8), Z = 208,000. Combined minimum total cost: Rs 379,000.

Example 4 — Radio Components LP (Problem Statement)

An electronic company is engaged in the production of two components C₁ and C₂ used in radio sets. Each unit of C₁ costs the company Rs 5 in wages and Rs 5 in material, while each of C₂ costs the company Rs 25 in wages and Rs 15 in material. The company sells both products on one‑period credit terms, but the company's labour and material expenses must be paid in cash. The selling price of C₁ is Rs 30 per unit and of C₂ it is Rs 70 per unit. Because of the strong monopoly of the company for these components, it is assumed that the company can sell at the prevailing prices as many units as it produces. The company's production capacity is, however, limited by two considerations. First, at the beginning of period 1, the company has an initial balance of Rs 4,000 (cash plus bank credit plus collections from past credit sales). Second, the company has available in each period 2,000 hours of machine time and 1,400 hours of assembly time. The production of each C₁ requires 3 hours of machine time and 2 hours of assembly time, whereas the production of each C₂ requires 2 hours of machine time and 3 hours of assembly time. Formulate this problem as an LP model so as to maximize the total profit to the company.

Resources / ConstraintsC₁C₂Total Availability
Budget (Rs)10/unit40/unitRs 4,000
Machine time3 hrs/unit2 hrs/unit2,000 hours
Assembly time2 hrs/unit3 hrs/unit1,400 hours
Selling price (Rs)3070—
Cost: wages + material (Rs)1040—

Decision variables: x₁, x₂ = units of C₁, C₂ produced.
Profit/unit = selling price − cost price = (30−10)x₁ + (70−40)x₂.
Maximize Z = 20x₁ + 30x₂
s.t. 10x₁+40x₂ ≤ 4000 (budget); 3x₁+2x₂ ≤ 2000 (machine time); 2x₁+3x₂ ≤ 1400 (assembly time); x₁,x₂ ≥ 0.
Note: like Example 2, this one has non‑binding constraints — the machine‑time and assembly‑time limits are both completely redundant here; the budget line alone shapes the whole feasible region (check for yourself: neither time line ever crosses the budget line for x₁,x₂≥0). Optimum at (x₁,x₂) = (400, 0), Z = 8,000 — all budget on C₁, with machine and assembly time both left well under capacity.
